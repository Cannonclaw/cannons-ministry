# The MCTV Playbook: Emulating a Place-Based DOOH Cash-Flow Empire

*A strategy brief for MCTV — a place-based digital-out-of-home (DOOH) venue-screen network, established and scaling, with the goal of a durable recurring-cash-flow business the owner keeps (not a build-to-sell exit).*

---

## TL;DR

The single best person to emulate is **François de Gaspé Beaubien**, sole owner of **Zoom Media** (in-gym / bar / restroom screen network, brand "GymTV"). He is the closest match to MCTV on **all three** dimensions that matter:

1. **Same medium** — screens inside venues he does *not* own (gyms, bars, restrooms).
2. **Same ownership goal** — he *bought the company in 2003 and remains the sole owner*, no VC or PE control, and grew it into the largest gym-based video network in the world (~5,000+ clubs, ~25,000+ screens, 35M+ monthly viewers).
3. **Same growth path a scaling regional operator can actually run** — he scaled by *rolling up small competitors* and *bolting on programmatic demand*, not by raising equity and selling out.

Three other operators are worth studying as *supporting* playbooks, each contributing one crucial lesson:

- **Doug Endsley — IndoorMedia / Register Tapes Unlimited** → the purest "own-it-forever cash-flow empire" (family-owned since 1989, ~$85M/yr est.). Teaches the **cash-flow discipline**: minimize capex, sell fragmented *local* advertisers on exclusivity, avoid concentration risk.
- **Mike DiFranza — Captivate Network** (elevator screens) → teaches the **venue-acquisition structure** (revenue-share + host equity) and **premium-audience positioning**.
- **David Leider — GSTV (Gas Station TV)** → teaches **make the screen an amenity, not a project**, and **build the measurement layer as a moat**.

The rest of this brief distills *how* they did it and turns it into a concrete plan for MCTV.

---

## Why François de Gaspé Beaubien / Zoom Media is the primary analog

Most celebrated DOOH founders (DiFranza at Captivate → sold to Gannett; Leider at GSTV → sold to private equity) built to **sell**. That is the opposite of MCTV's stated goal. de Gaspé Beaubien did the rare thing: he built an *extraordinary* place-based screen business **and kept it**, privately, for 20+ years.

**What he did:**
- **Acquired an existing network (2003) and consolidated a fragmented category.** Rather than raise venture money, he rolled up smaller in-gym networks (ClubCom, Gym Television Ltd., Wellness Health Education Network) to become the category leader while retaining sole ownership.
- **Made the screen an amenity the venue actively wants.** Zoom installs and operates the screens *inside gyms/bars it doesn't own*, gives the club free entertainment/music/fitness content that improves the member experience, and **shares ad revenue with the club** — "extra revenue without any additional work." Zoom owns the hardware, content, and playback; the venue provides the audience and the wall space.
- **Sold a scarce, premium demographic.** A captive, distraction-limited audience (people on cardio machines) that CPG, apparel, auto, and financial advertisers pay up for. Proof points like "within two hours of a gym visit, 95% of members run errands and 72% researched/bought a product they saw advertised in-gym," backed by Kantar measurement.
- **Bolted on programmatic to fill inventory automatically.** Partnerships with the major DOOH SSPs (VIOOH, Vistar, Hivestack) route national DSP budgets onto his screens without a national sales army — so once screens are installed, incremental ad revenue is nearly pure margin.

**Why it's a cash-flow machine:** once the screens are in, the marginal cost of an additional impression is near zero; the venue is happy because it gets free content + a revenue check; the demographic is scarce enough to command premium CPMs; and programmatic fills whatever the local sales team doesn't sell.

*Verification note: Zoom Media is private; exact revenue-share percentages, CPMs, and margins are not publicly disclosed. Footprint figures are from company/trade sources.*

## The transferable playbook — 5 principles the "keep-it" winners share

Across all four operators, the businesses that stayed private and cash-rich share the same DNA. This is the core of what MCTV should emulate.

### 1. Minimize capex per venue — it is the #1 predictor of who stays independent
The clearest pattern in the research: the *less hardware* an operator carried, the more durable and self-funding the business.

> Paper (IndoorMedia, RTN) > printed frames (Newad) > owned digital screens (Zoom) > big freestanding kiosks (Adspace, which struggled for years and had to raise capital).

MCTV already carries screens, so the lesson is: **push hardware cost down and out** — cheap plug-and-play players, cellular connectivity (Captivate moved to cellular to cut install complexity), and wherever possible **ride equipment the venue is already buying** (GSTV's masterstroke: the screen came embedded in the fuel dispenser the retailer was replacing anyway, so it was an amenity, not an install project).

### 2. Give the venue something concrete — not just the promise of a revenue check
The stickiest host relationships hand the venue a real, tangible benefit:
- **IndoorMedia**: free register-tape *paper* — a line-item cost the store already pays. No rev-share to negotiate, no hardware.
- **GSTV**: free "Retailer Promotional Ads" — the retailer's *own* marketing channel to drive c-store traffic, handed to them free.
- **Zoom / Captivate**: free content that improves the member/tenant experience + building-specific messaging.

For MCTV: the host deal should be **zero-cost and zero-effort for the venue**, and ideally give them a free promotional slot ("your business, on your own screens, free") *plus* a revenue share. That combination is why these contracts renew and stay exclusive.

### 3. Sell fragmented *local* advertisers on exclusivity + measurable ROI — that's what makes it a cash-flow business
This is the single most important lever for the "keep-it, live-off-it" goal.
- The durable private operators (IndoorMedia, RTN) sell **thousands of renewing 12–24-week local contracts** with a **geographic/category exclusive** ("no competitor can buy this venue"). Result: **pricing power, no customer-concentration risk, recurring cash flow.**
- The operators who chased **national/pharma money** (AccentHealth) got pulled toward an **exit** and single-advertiser dependence.

For MCTV: your bread-and-butter should be **local businesses** (dentists, roofers, restaurants, salons, auto) buying an *exclusive* on a venue or cluster on 12–24-week auto-renewing terms. Layer national/programmatic on *top* to fill unsold inventory — but the local book is the durable core you live off.

### 4. Scale with commissioned salespeople and small tuck-in acquisitions — not equity
- IndoorMedia bootstrapped from a franchisee's side-need into the national leader, scaling on a **commission-funded local sales force**.
- de Gaspé Beaubien **rolled up small competitors** while keeping sole ownership.

For MCTV: the scaling input is **feet-on-the-street reps paid out of revenue**, plus **buying out small local screen operators** in adjacent venues/markets — not a capital raise that dilutes the very ownership you want to keep.

### 5. Exclusivity is the moat — on both sides
Exclusive *venue* contracts (only your screens in that gym/lobby/clinic) **and** exclusive *advertiser* geographies (only one dentist per venue-cluster). That two-sided lock is what makes the cash flow durable and defensible.

**And the premium multiplier (from Captivate + GSTV):** you can charge *far* higher CPMs if you (a) curate genuinely useful silent/short content that earns attention, and (b) **prove the audience with third-party measurement** (impressions + brand-lift / foot-traffic / sales-lift studies). Measurement is not a marketing afterthought — it's what unlocks premium rates and is itself a moat.

## The emulation plan for MCTV

Mapped to your stage (established & scaling) and goal (recurring cash-flow empire you keep).

### Phase 1 — Lock the foundation (0–90 days)
1. **Restructure every host deal to the "amenity + exclusive" standard:** zero cost / zero effort to the venue, a free self-promo slot for the venue, a revenue-share, **and a written exclusivity clause** (only MCTV screens in that venue). Re-paper existing venues onto this template as they renew.
2. **Instrument the network for measurement.** Standardize on impression counting (dwell × traffic), and line up at least one third-party validation angle (a measurement partner, or documented foot-traffic/sales-lift case studies with 2–3 friendly advertisers). This is the price of premium CPMs.
3. **Build the local rate card around exclusivity.** Package inventory as *exclusive local category sponsorships* on 12–24-week auto-renew terms, not one-off impressions. Price on ROI/exclusivity, not cheap CPM.

### Phase 2 — Turn on the two revenue engines (90–180 days)
4. **Stand up a commission-funded local sales motion.** Reps paid mostly on commission, selling exclusive local sponsorships venue-by-venue. Target the fragmented local base (the renewing book is your cash flow).
5. **Connect unsold inventory to programmatic.** Integrate with a DOOH SSP (Vistar, Broadsign/Place Exchange, or Hivestack) so national brand budgets fill whatever local sales doesn't — near-pure-margin dollars on already-installed screens.
6. **Curate content that earns attention.** Short, glanceable, silent-friendly, genuinely useful local content (news/weather/local info) interleaved with ads — the "attention engine" that makes the ad slot sellable (Captivate/GSTV's core content lesson).

### Phase 3 — Consolidate and compound (180+ days)
7. **Land a lighthouse anchor.** Court the largest local venue group/chain in your category as a reference account (Captivate's REIT-portfolio move; GSTV's Murphy USA / Walmart-adjacent move) — one signature that delivers many venues and instant credibility.
8. **Roll up small local screen operators** in adjacent venue types/markets — the de Gaspé Beaubien move — funded by cash flow, keeping ownership intact.
9. **Push hardware cost down** (cheap players, cellular connectivity, ride venue equipment refresh cycles) so each new venue is cheap to light up and stays high-margin.

### What to deliberately *avoid* (the anti-patterns from the research)
- **Don't chase national/pharma-scale dependence** at the expense of the local book — it pulls you toward an exit and concentration risk (AccentHealth).
- **Don't over-invest in expensive freestanding hardware** in declining-traffic venues (Adspace/malls — the most capital-hungry, least durable end).
- **Don't take dilutive capital** to grow — the whole point is to keep it. Grow on commissions + cash-funded tuck-ins.

## Open questions to tailor this to MCTV

To turn this into a specific 90-day action list, I need to know:
1. **Venue types & footprint:** what venues are your screens in today, how many screens/venues, and in what geography?
2. **Current host deals:** do you pay venues, rev-share, or give free screens? Any exclusivity clauses today?
3. **How you sell now:** local direct, national, programmatic, or a mix? Roughly what CPM / package price?
4. **Measurement:** do you count impressions / prove audience today, and how?
5. **Team:** do you have a sales force, and is it commission-based?

Answer those and we'll convert this playbook into MCTV's specific numbers, rate card, host-deal template, and acquisition target list.
